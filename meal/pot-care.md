# Pot care — cleaning a scorched stainless pot

For the Joyoung stainless inner pot (and any stainless pot) after a sear
scorches the base — browned ring, caramelised patch, burnt ginger, etc.
The scorch sits *on* the steel, not in it: this is a cleaning job, not damage.
No warping or pitting comes from a few minutes of searing heat.

First used 12 Nov 2026 (Day 18 papaya fish soup sear).

## Steps

1. **Tip out the debris** — burnt ginger, loose bits, anything not stuck down.
2. **Fill the pot about a third full of hot water**, add **1 tablespoon of
   baking soda**.
3. **Simmer on the gas ring for 5–10 minutes** — the carbon softens and
   starts lifting on its own.
4. **Dark patch**: after pouring the water out, smear a **thick paste of
   baking soda + a little water** over it and leave it **10 minutes**.
5. **Scrub with a non-scratch sponge** — the brown ring should wipe off; the
   dark patch takes a bit of pressure, not force.
6. **Rinse and dry.**

## Notes

- **Heat tint** (blue/rainbow sheen on the steel): cosmetic only, harmless.
  A wipe with vinegar removes it.
- **Shortcut when there's no rush**: fill with hot water and soak through the
  meal first — a long soak does half the work before any scrubbing.
- **Escalation for a stubborn patch**: repeat the baking soda simmer, or leave
  the paste on longer (an hour, or overnight). Reach for these before anything
  abrasive.
- **Avoid**: steel wool as a first resort — it scratches the finish and makes
  future scorch stick harder.
