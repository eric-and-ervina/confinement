## 📊 Recipe scores — how to read them

Every recipe carries three scores out of 5, so you can pick by how tired you are and by what she needs that day.

| Score | What it measures | 5 means | 3 means |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ease** | **How effortless it is** — active minutes at the counter and how much attention it needs, on this exact gear | load it, press the button, walk away (or one tray / 2 minutes of assembly) | a short sear or a second stage: real timing and attention, still beginner-safe |
| **Taste** | How good the finished dish is likely to be | a dish you'd cook again after the month is over | plain and functional — fine warm, not exciting |
| **Confinement** | How well it serves *her*: warm, ginger/sesame base, protein / iron / calcium / DHA, easy digestion, lactation, and the plan's safety rules | a core confinement dish — nourishing and safe as often as she likes | a treat or a filler — fine occasionally, not a staple |

Two honest caveats. These are judgement calls, not lab data, and **nothing here scores below 3** — it is all beginner-safe. And **Ease is about effort, not skill**: a steamed egg custard is low-effort but needs technique (3), while a 45-minute braise you barely touch is high-effort-efficiency (4) — a long cook time is not the same as hard work. The **Confinement** score already accounts for the wine, dang gui and angkak caveats noted in each recipe, so a 4 can still be an excellent dish that just needs the feed-first rule.

> 🔧 **开盖收汁 is a sauce reducer, not a frying pan — so this book splits the job.** The button is built to hold a gentle simmer with the lid off, after a pressure cycle, until the sauce thickens. That is exactly what it is good at. It is *not* a sear mode: in practice it does not get hot enough to brown meat properly, and the pot is too deep and narrow for a 500 g batch, so food added cold releases water and goes grey instead of golden.
>
> So: **sear and brown in the frying pan on medium → deglaze the pan with the recipe's wine or water → tip everything into the cooker → close and run the preset → use 开盖收汁 for the final reduction.** One extra pan, and the deglaze leaves it nearly clean.
>
> Frying *aromatics only* — ginger in oil, no meat, no browning wanted — is fine in the pot. Test **开盖收汁** once after your first pressure cook: open the lid, add a splash of water, press the button — if it simmers, the reduction step works as written. If it does nothing on your model, reduce the sauce in the frying pan instead; nothing else changes.

### Steaming in a frying pan — the one technique to get right

Your rack lives inside a **frying pan**, not a deep pot, so the water is shallow. That's fine — with four habits:

1. **Water to just below the rack** (~2 cm). The plate of food sits *on* the rack, above the waterline.
2. **Always lid it** — a tight pan lid, or a plate or foil that overhangs the rim. Escaping steam is cooking time lost, and it's why pan-steaming gets a bad name.
3. **Top up at the 10-minute mark** for anything steaming longer than 12 min: kettle in one hand, pour *boiling* water down the inside edge, away from the food. Never cold water, and never let it run dry.
4. **Keep the flame under the pan**, not licking up the sides.

With a shallow pan, **fish and egg custard (8–12 min) are the sweet spot**; long steams are the thing to avoid. So in this book:

- the **20-minute sweet potato** goes in the oven on the **红薯** preset (recipe 68),
- **chicken thighs** get cut into strips (12–15 min) or poached in the pressure cooker (recipes 31–32),
- nothing steams for more than ~15 min in the pan.

> **The AUX oven can't steam.** Its panel is bake/fry presets only (烤鱼, 烤鸡, 红薯…) — there is no 蒸 mode, and a hot-air oven can't hold 100 °C steam anyway. Every "steamed" dish here is the **pan + rack + lid**. That's the classic method regardless: water boils, plate on the rack, lid on, timer runs.

### The gas hob's only job
The hob appears in **three safe roles only** — nothing with hot-oil splatter or a scary high flame:
1. **Boiling water** — under the steam rack, and for blanching.
2. **Gentle simmering** — broth, noodles, poached eggs, the red-date tea.
3. **Gentle medium-heat pan work** — the vegetable sides, plus the short sear (ginger, meat, fish bones) that starts a braise or soup. Heat on *medium* (never roaring), meat patted dry, so it browns without spitting (see the Greens section and the braise note).

Everything that needs real time under heat (rice, soups, braises, fish) happens **set-and-forget** in the **pressure cooker** or the **air-fryer oven**.

### ⏰ The overnight trick (预约) — the least effort in this book

**预约** on the pressure cooker is the single best feature in the house for a newborn month. Load it at night, wake up to breakfast:

1. **10.30 pm:** rice + water + ginger and/or red dates in the pot, lid on, **预约** set so it's ready around 6.30 am (that's your 8 hours).
2. **Morning:** it's done and sitting on **保温**. Boil the eggs in the pan while you're at it — breakfast is two minutes of work.
3. Same trick for congee (recipes 9–12), the red-date/longan base for the oatmeal (13 — just add oats in the morning) and the red-date tea (6).

Two rules: **keep the delay under ~8 hours**, and **grains, water and dried herbs only** — never meat, fish or egg in a delayed pot, which would sit warm for hours before cooking. And check your model's 预约 once: if it counts down *to start* rather than *to finish*, set it to (ready-time − cook-time) and it lands the same way.

# ⭐ Signature Dishes

## 1. Sesame Oil Chicken (麻油鸡)

*Appears: days 8, 14, 15, 18, 22, 26*

**Scores — Ease 4/5 · Taste 5/5 · Confinement 5/5** · *Pan sear, one-pot finish; the definitive confinement chicken. Wine, so feed-first.*

**Ingredients**
- 500 g chicken (prefer 甘榜/camp chicken or bone-in thighs)
- 3 tbsp sesame oil (use roasted, not the pale cooking kind)
- 8–10 slices old ginger (老姜)
- 150 ml rice wine (米酒/黄酒) + water to cover
- 1 tbsp brown sugar (optional)

**Method (pan sear → pressure cooker)**
1. Pat the chicken dry. Warm the sesame oil in the frying pan on **medium**, fry the ginger slices until golden and fragrant (5–7 min) — this is where the flavour lives.
2. Add the chicken and sear until golden on all sides; don't crowd the pan — two batches beats one steamed batch.
3. Pour in the rice wine and scrape the browned bits off the pan, then tip the chicken, ginger and liquid into the pressure cooker. Add enough water to barely cover.
4. Close the lid, select **鸡鸭肉** and cook ~25–30 min until tender.
5. Quick or natural release; if the sauce is loose, **开盖收汁** 3–5 min. Add sugar if you like. Serve with rice or 面线.

**Trick:** the pan gives you the golden crust the dish is named for; the cooker gives you the tender, hands-off finish. Deglazing with the wine means none of that browned flavour stays behind in the pan.

> ⚠️ **Alcohol note (breastfeeding):** alcohol does **not** boost milk supply — and it passes into breastmilk — so treat the wine as flavour and tradition, not a supply tool. A pressure-cook finish still leaves some alcohol; for peace of mind, use low-alcohol confinement-brewed wine (米酒), or swap some of the wine for extra water/stock. **Practical rule:** have her feed/pump just before the meal, or wait ~2 hours after it — most of this plan's wine dishes land at lunch, so feed-first is the realistic version (cooking them at dinner when you can is still the easy win).

## 2. Green Papaya Fish Soup (青木瓜鱼汤) ⭐ Traditional Galactagogue

*Appears: days 15, 16, 18, 21, 26*

**Scores — Ease 3/5 · Taste 4/5 · Confinement 5/5** · *Searing the fish is the only fiddly bit; milky broth and the traditional milk booster.*

**Ingredients**
- 400 g fish (snakehead 生鱼, threadfin 午鱼, or batang 马鲛) — fillets + bones
- 1 semi-ripe green papaya, peeled, seeded, chunked
- 6–8 slices old ginger
- 1.5 L boiling water (this is the milky-broth secret)
- Salt to taste (light)

**Method (pan-fried fish → pressure cooker)**
1. Pat the fish dry — wet fish won't brown. Heat a little sesame oil in the frying pan on **medium**; fry the ginger until fragrant, then the **bones** until lightly golden. Keep the fillets aside. In a stainless pan, leave each piece untouched for 30 sec before you try to move it.
2. Pour a ladle of the **boiling** water into the hot pan — it will hiss, emulsify and lift the browned bits; scrape it in. Tip the bones, ginger and that liquid into the pressure cooker, then add the rest of the boiling water. The broth turns milky white.
3. Add the papaya chunks. Close the lid, select **美味汤**, cook ~20–25 min until papaya is soft.
4. Quick-release, open the lid, slide in the fillet pieces and cook **开盖收汁** 3–5 min until just opaque — they stay whole instead of dissolving. Season lightly, serve hot.

**Trick:** frying the fish + adding boiling water = the white, milky colour. Never add cold water.

> 💡 **On the "lactation booster" label:** green papaya fish soup is a *traditional* galactagogue, but the evidence is anecdotal. What actually has evidence behind it: **oatmeal, adequate total fluids (~2–3 L/day of warm liquids), and frequent emptying of the breast** — more feeds/pumps = more supply. It's a lovely, nourishing soup regardless — serve it with pride, promise a bit less.

## 3. Herbal Black Chicken Soup (药材鸡汤)

*Appears: days 17, 19, 23, 24, 28*

**Scores — Ease 5/5 · Taste 4/5 · Confinement 5/5** · *Blanch, dump, press 美味汤; mild herbal taste. Pause the dang gui if bleeding.*

**Ingredients**
- 1 black chicken (Silkie 乌鸡), chopped
- Herbal pack — pick one:
  - **Pre-packed:** 1 confinement herbal pack (from Eu Yan Sang, Ma Kuang, or any Chinese medical hall), or
  - **Assembled yourself:** the DIY pack in the table below
- 8 slices ginger
- 2 L water
- 1 tbsp rice wine (optional)

**DIY herbal pack (for 1 pot / 2 L)**

| Herb | Amount | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Dang gui 当归 (slices) | 12 g (about 4–6 slices) | Blood-building; the classic confinement herb |
| Astragalus 黄芪 (slices) | 15 g (a small handful) | Qi/energy support — safe throughout |
| Red dates 红枣 | 8, pitted | Sweetness + blood-building |
| Dried longan 龙眼 | 10 | Natural sweetness, energy |
| Goji 枸杞 | 1 tbsp | Added at the very end, last 10 min |

**Method (pressure cooker — the easiest big soup you'll make)**
1. Blanch the chicken in boiling water 2 min, drain (removes scum).
2. **Assemble the pack:** rinse the herbs (except goji). Either tie them in a muslin bag/cloth, or add them loose — if loose, just scoop the big slices out before serving.
3. Add the chicken, herbs, ginger and 2 L water to the pressure cooker. Close the lid, select **美味汤**, cook ~25–30 min (deep, long-simmer flavour in a fraction of the stove time).
4. Natural release, then open and stir in the goji; lid back on and leave 5 min — the residual heat plumps them without dulling the colour. (Or just add them at the start if you'd rather not open the pot.)
5. Season lightly. Remove the herb bag (or scoop out the large pieces). Drink the soup, eat the chicken.

**Shortcut:** pre-packed confinement herbal packs (Eu Yan Sang, Ma Kuang, any Chinese medical hall) are built for exactly this — zero measuring. If using one, follow the pack's weight (typically one pack per 2 L), and add goji at the end if it isn't already inside.

**Batch tip:** this soup is on the plan 5× (days 17, 19, 23, 24, 28) — cook a double batch once and freeze half in meal-size portions. Reheat gently; it tastes even better the next day.

> ⚠️ **Dang gui (当归) caution:** dang gui is a blood-moving herb. If lochia is still heavy — or bleeding restarts — pause the dang gui until it settles. Simply leave it out (or swap in a little more astragalus) — astragalus and red dates are safe throughout.

## 8. Steamed Fish with Ginger (姜丝蒸鱼)

*Appears: days 1, 5, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14, 16, 18, 28 — your most-used fish method*

**Scores — Ease 4/5 · Taste 5/5 · Confinement 5/5** · *Boil, plate, timer; light, clean protein — the most-used fish dish.*

**Ingredients**
- 1 whole fish (snapper, pomfret, seabass) or thick fillet
- 6 slices ginger, cut into fine strips
- 1 spring onion, shredded
- 1 tbsp light soy sauce
- 1 tsp sesame oil (drizzle at the end)

**Method (frying pan + rack + lid — the foolproof way)**
1. Rack into the **frying pan**, water to just below it (~2 cm), lid on, bring to a rolling boil.
2. Score the fish, stuff ginger strips into the cuts and cavity. Place on a heatproof plate on the rack, lid back on.
3. Steam over high heat 8–12 min (10 min per 2.5 cm thickness) — under 12 min, so no top-up needed.
4. Discard the watery fish juice, top with fresh ginger + spring onion, drizzle soy + hot sesame oil (heat the oil in a metal ladle over the flame, or in the microwave).

# 🍚 Congee, Porridge & Oatmeal

> **All of these run on the pressure cooker's 浓香粥 (or 快速粥) — combine the ingredients, press the button, walk away.** Times are approximate; the machine handles the timing. And every one of them can be loaded the night before on **预约**.

## 9. Fish Congee (鱼粥)

*Appears: day 2*

**Scores — Ease 4/5 · Taste 4/5 · Confinement 5/5** · *Two components (congee + fish), both hands-off; gentle week-1 protein.*

**Ingredients**
- 100 g rice
- 1 L water
- 150 g threadfin (午鱼) fillet, or any white fish
- 6 slices ginger
- 1 tsp sesame oil
- Salt & white pepper to taste
- Spring onion, to finish

**Method**
1. Rinse rice. Put rice + water + ginger in the pressure cooker, select **浓香粥**, cook until thick (machine timing).
2. Meanwhile **烤鱼** preset on the AUX oven, 10–12 min (keeps the fish sweet and tender), or poach it in the pan; flake into chunks.
3. Fold the fish into the congee, season, let sit covered 5 min.
4. Top with spring onion, serve hot.

**Trick:** fish cooked separately and folded in at the end keeps it sweet and tender, not mushy.

## 13. Red Date & Longan Oatmeal (红枣龙眼燕麦粥) ⭐ Lactation Breakfast

*Appears: days 4, 5, 7, 10, 14, 15, 17, 19, 23, 26 — confirmed 30 Oct 2026: Ervina ate the Day 4 bowl and is fine with oatmeal, so the proposed oatmeal swap is retired and Day 5 joins this list*

**Scores — Ease 4/5 · Taste 4/5 · Confinement 5/5** · *12 minutes at the pan with a stir; oats + warm fluids = the evidence-based lactation breakfast.*

**Ingredients**
- 60 g rolled oats
- 500 ml water (or half water / half milk if she tolerates it)
- 6 red dates, pitted
- 8 dried longan
- 1 egg (optional)
- Brown sugar to taste

**Method (frying pan, 12 minutes)**
1. Simmer red dates + longan in the water ~10 min — that's the sweet base.
2. Add oats, cook 5–7 min on low, stirring, until thick and creamy.
3. Sweeten lightly. Top with a soft-boiled egg — that egg is the protein, so it isn't optional.

> 💡 **Not a pressure-cooker dish.** Oats are five minutes on the hob and they foam under pressure — leave the machine for rice, congee and soup.

## 18. Light Fish Soup (清鱼汤)

*Appears: days 2, 4, 7, 9, 13, 20, 25 (the plan's "mild / white / clear fish soup")*

**Scores — Ease 4/5 · Taste 4/5 · Confinement 5/5** · *The boiling-water trick takes practice; light, easily digested fish protein.*

- 300 g fish bones + 100 g fillet (threadfin, snapper, or seabass)
- 6–8 slices old ginger
- 1 L **boiling** water
- 1 tsp sesame oil
- Salt & white pepper to taste
- Spring onion, to finish

# 🥚 Steamed Dishes

> **All steamed dishes use the pan + rack + lid** (the setup in [Your 4 cooking tools](#-your-4-cooking-tools-exactly-these)). It's as easy as it sounds: water boils under the rack, the plate sits on the rack, the lid stays on, a timer runs. Two rules for this shallow pan: **lid on**, and **top up with boiling water at the 10-minute mark** for anything over 12 min.

## 30. Steamed Egg Custard (蒸水蛋)

*Appears: day 2*

**Scores — Ease 3/5 · Taste 4/5 · Confinement 5/5** · *Straining + low heat + covering is real technique; silky protein when it works.*

**Ingredients**
- 2 eggs
- 150 ml warm water or light broth (about 1.5× the egg volume)
- Pinch of salt
- 1 tsp light soy sauce
- Few drops sesame oil
- Spring onion, to finish

**Method (pan + rack + lid)**
1. Rack into the pan, water just below it, lid on, bring to a rolling boil.
2. Whisk eggs + warm water + salt until frothy.
3. Strain through a sieve into a shallow bowl (this is what makes it silky).
4. Cover the bowl with a plate or cling wrap — stops condensation from dimpling the surface.
5. Steam on **medium-low** heat 10–12 min until just set with a gentle wobble. (Keep the heat gentle — full-rolling-boil steam makes it spongy.)
6. Drizzle soy + sesame oil, top with spring onion, serve warm.

**Trick:** low heat + covered + strained = restaurant-smooth custard. Over-steaming makes it spongy.

# 🍖 Braises (Pressure Cooker)

> **Every braise = sear in the pan → deglaze → close and use 排骨 (牛羊肉 for beef) → 开盖收汁 to finish.** ~30–45 min set-and-forget for what used to be 1–1.5 h of stove-watching.
>
> 🔧 **Two things worth knowing.** (1) **开盖收汁 reduces the sauce; it doesn't sear.** It holds a simmer, so cold meat added to it goes grey. Brown in the pan, then deglaze with the recipe's water or wine and tip it all in — the fond *is* the flavour (see [the note at the top](#the-buttons-as-printed-on-your-machines)). (2) Every preset defaults to its own time, so **set the minutes with 时间/温度 + +/−** to the number in the recipe.

## 69. Angkak Chicken (红曲米鸡)

*Appears: days 12, 25 (swaps in for the yellow wine chicken)*

**Scores — Ease 3/5 · Taste 5/5 · Confinement 4/5** · *Paste step + sear + press + reduce; delicious, but 红曲 is blood-moving — pause if lochia is heavy.*

**Ingredients**
- 500 g chicken, chopped (甘榜/camp chicken or bone-in thighs)
- **1–2 tsp angkak (红曲米)** — 1 tsp = rosy, 2 tsp = deep red 红糟 look
- 2 tbsp sesame oil (roasted)
- 8–10 slices old ginger (老姜)
- 150 ml rice wine (米酒/黄酒) — **not arak**
- 100 ml water or light chicken broth (recipe 16)
- 1 tsp light soy sauce
- 1 tsp brown sugar (optional)
- 50 g black fungus (soaked, optional) or 4–6 dried mushrooms (soaked)

**Method (pan sear → pressure cooker)**
1. **Make the angkak paste:** put the angkak in a small bowl with 2 tbsp of the rice wine, mash with the back of a spoon into a smooth red paste (30 sec). This is what gives an even colour instead of speckled rice grains stuck to the chicken.
2. Pat the chicken dry. Warm the sesame oil in the frying pan on **medium**.
3. Fry the ginger slices until golden and fragrant (5–7 min) — the flavour base, same as the sesame oil chicken.
4. Add the chicken, sear until golden on all sides.
5. Spoon in the angkak paste and toss 1 min until every piece is coated red — the colour sets now, in the hot oil.
6. Add the fungus/mushrooms, the rest of the wine and the water, and scrape the pan. Tip everything into the pressure cooker, then add the soy and sugar.
7. Close the lid, select **鸡鸭肉** and cook ~20–25 min until tender.
8. Natural release, then **开盖收汁** 3–5 min to a glossy red sauce. Serve hot over rice or 面线.

**Trick:** mash the angkak into a paste first. Sprinkled in dry, it colours unevenly and leaves a slightly gritty, medicinal edge — as a paste it turns into a proper red braise.

**If it's too strong:** angkak is fermented, so it tastes gently sour-savoury. Too much reads as "medicinal" — next pot, drop to 1 tsp rather than adding more sugar.

**No-wine version:** swap the 150 ml wine for 150 ml chicken broth + 1 tsp rice vinegar. You lose a little warmth but keep the red, savoury character — and it's completely alcohol-free.

> ⚠️ **Alcohol note (breastfeeding):** as with every wine dish here — the wine is flavour and tradition, **not** a supply booster, and alcohol passes into breastmilk. Use low-alcohol confinement 米酒 and have her feed/pump just before the meal (or wait ~2 hours after it) — this is a lunch dish on both days, so feed-first is the one you'll use. And **do not use arak** for this dish: it's a distilled 30–40% spirit, and a short braise does not cook that off.

> ⚠️ **Angkak note (why only 1–2 tsp):** angkak contains monacolin K — the same compound as the cholesterol drug lovastatin — so at supplement doses (grams a day) it behaves like a medicine. As a teaspoon in a braise it's just a seasoning, which is exactly how it's meant to be used. Two extra cautions: buy **sealed, properly labelled** packs (cheap loose angkak can carry citrinin, a mould toxin), and **pause it if her lochia is heavy or bleeding restarts** — 红曲 moves blood, like dang gui. If she's on any blood thinner or statin, ask her doctor first; it's a small ingredient, not worth an interaction.
