# 🍲 28-Day Confinement (坐月子) Meal Plan — Singapore

A complete home-cooking plan for a husband preparing 28 days of postpartum meals for his wife, using ingredients readily available in Singapore.

> ⚕️ **Medical note — please read.** This is a home-cooking plan, not medical advice. Confinement traditions and postpartum nutrition vary, and individual needs differ (wound healing, bleeding, medications, allergies, existing conditions). Please check with your wife's **OB/GYN, a lactation consultant, or a dietitian** before starting, and treat the "avoid/limit" card as general guidance rather than a prescription. **Seek medical care promptly** — don't just adjust the menu — if she has heavy bleeding, fever/chills or worsening pain, or a red, hot, painful breast with flu-like symptoms (possible mastitis).

## The 4 Golden Rules

1. **Everything warm.** No raw salads, no iced drinks, no cold water. All vegetables cooked. Plain water is replaced by warm red date/longan tea — keep a thermos on her bedside.
2. **Ginger + sesame oil + rice wine** are your base. Old ginger (老姜) goes into nearly every savoury dish.
3. **Small frequent meals.** 3 meals + 2 snacks/teas a day beats 3 big meals.
4. **No innards, no trotters** — swapped for pork ribs/collar/lean pork, chicken, fish, beef, eggs. Protein and collagen needs are fully covered without them.

## 🍎 Nutrition refinements (now built into the day tables)

These fill the gaps the original plan (deliberately kept simple) skipped over — and they are now **reflected in the 28‑day day tables** (and the matching recipe notes), so the day-by-day plan actually delivers them.

1. **Warm fruit every day — add as a snack.** Postpartum constipation is common, and fruit's fibre + vitamin C (which also helps iron absorption) makes a real difference. Warm fits the "everything warm" rule: stewed/poached apple or pear (with ginger + a red date), banana at room temperature (Singapore room temp is fine), or a little papaya. **Aim for at least 1 portion/day.**
2. **Boost calcium — properly.** Breastfeeding needs ~1000–1300 mg/day, and the plan as written lands at roughly **300–450 mg**: unfortified 豆奶 is only ~30–75 mg/100 ml (a cup is a drink, not a calcium source), tofu appears on only 4 days, and **spinach — the most-used green — is oxalate-rich and barely counts**. Fixed by stacking **two or three of these on every day** (one cup of soy milk is only ~300 mg, so a single hit can't cover it): **1 cup fortified/high-calcium milk or soy milk (~300 mg)**, **calcium-set tofu 150–200 g (~250 mg)**, **20 g ikan bilis in a soup (~250–300 mg)**, **100 g kai lan / choy sum / bok choy (~100–150 mg)**. If she isn't near ~1000 mg, **calcium 500–600 mg + vitamin D3** is the simple backstop — ask her doctor.
3. **Blood-building week = more red meat.** Beef now appears on days 7, **9**, 11 and 25 — day 9's pork lunch is swapped for **beef w/ ginger & onion** (gentle pan). Beef's iron absorbs better than pork's, so pair any red meat with a vitamin-C veg (broccoli, kai lan).
4. **DHA: salmon twice a week.** Salmon now appears on days 4, **17, 19** (week 3) and **24, 27** (week 4) — **twice a week** in weeks 3–4 for DHA (baby brain development); batang (马鲛) can stand in for one of those — but keep it to small fish, max ~once a week (see the limit card).
5. **Watch the sweetness.** Most sweet dishes already say "to taste — use as little sugar as possible." Also, with old ginger + sesame oil + longan daily, some people get 热气 (heaty): if she develops a sore throat or mouth ulcers, ease off the ginger and longan.

### Two things the menu can't do

6. **Keep her supplements going.** The postpartum month is not the time to drop the prenatal vitamin — folate, iron, iodine and vitamin D still matter, and a month indoors is exactly when vitamin D runs low. If she's exclusively breastfeeding, **baby also needs 400 IU vitamin D drops daily** (separate from anything she takes) — confirm with the paediatrician.
7. **Run one plan, not both.** This folder and [`../tcm/`](../tcm/README.md) share most of the same meals, and the TCM track adds 生化汤, 四物/八珍 and 杜仲 on top. Stack them and she'd be on five dang gui pots *and* a daily decoction — pick one, with a TCM physician's sign-off if it's the herb-forward one.

## Quick answers

- **What should I cook first?** **Day 3 dinner, Wed 28 Oct: rice noodles with chicken & bok choy in warm broth** — the first home-cooked meal (discharge is after ~11am–12pm, so lunch is hospital/in transit unless the Day 3 soup was pre-made). Then run the plan from Day 4 breakfast as written. Days 1–2 were hospital days — don't restart or back-fill them.
- **What boosts milk supply?** The evidence-based trio: oatmeal most days, ~2–3 L/day of warm fluids, and frequent feeding/pumping. Traditional boosters (green papaya fish soup, black sesame) are nourishing additions, but their galactagogue effect is anecdotal.
- **What if she gets bored?** Swap any fish for another fish, any leafy green for another leafy green — the structure stays the same.
- **Alcohol in rice wine?** Wine doesn't boost milk supply — it's for flavour and tradition — and alcohol passes into breastmilk. Long simmers (60+ min) reduce alcohol to trace levels. Short simmer (20–30 min) retains ~30%, so for shorter dishes use a low-alcohol confinement-brewed 米酒, or simmer longer — see the ⚠️ notes in recipes.md. Practical rule: **feed/pump just before the meal, or wait ~2 hours after it** — most of the plan's wine dishes sit at lunch (the 🍶 marks in [`meal-plan.md`](meal-plan.md)), so the feed-first version is the one you'll actually use. Cooking them at dinner, when you can, is still the easy win. **Arak is a different animal** — a distilled 30–40% spirit, not a cooking wine, so it's off the menu this month; make ayam angkak with 米酒/黄酒 instead.
