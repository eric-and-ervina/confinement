Three meals a day (breakfast / lunch / dinner), plus snacks and warm teas between meals.

### Day 3 (Wed 28 Oct) — lunch IS the first home meal (confirmed)

**Confirmed 27 Oct: the hospital provides breakfast only on discharge day — no hospital lunch.** So lunch must be cooked at home, and it is the first home-cooked meal. Discharge is after ~11am–12pm, so the whole lunch is designed to be **on the table ~20 min after walking in** — which means most of it happens the night before:

**Day 3 lunch as written:** rice + **baked pomfret on the 烤鱼 preset ([recipe 34](recipes.md#34-air-fried-pomfret-with-ginger-姜烤鲳鱼))** + **stir-fried lettuce ([recipe 54](recipes.md#54-stir-fried-lettuce-炒生菜))** + **pork rib-corn soup ([recipe 24](recipes.md#24-pork-rib-corn-soup-light-玉米排骨汤))**.

**Tue 27 Oct night — do these (this is what makes tomorrow work):**
1. **Cook the pork rib-corn soup in full** (blanch ribs, **美味汤** 25–30 min), cool, fridge in its pot/container. It reheats in ~5–10 min and tastes better the next day.
2. **Buy / prep the pomfret:** cleaned and scored from the market, keep raw in the fridge (salt and sesame oil go on tomorrow, just before baking — not overnight).
3. **Wash and drain the lettuce**, fridge in a covered container, so tomorrow it's a straight 1–2 min toss.
4. Measure out the rice, ready in the cooker pot.

**Wed 28 Oct — sequence:**
1. **Before leaving for Thomson:** load rice + water in the Joyoung and set **预约** to finish around **1pm** (adjust to your pickup time — cooked rice holds warm safely if you're later; grains + water only is exactly what 预约 is for). Bring a thermos of warm red date tea (and a warm snack, e.g. banana) for the ride home — breakfast was hours ago by then.
2. **On arrival home, in this order:** (a) soup on to reheat — she can start on the hot soup first while the rest finishes; (b) pomfret salted, oiled, ginger on, into the AUX oven on **烤鱼**, 12–18 min; (c) lettuce tossed 1–2 min at the end. Rice is already done.
3. **Day 3 dinner as written** in the evening: rice noodles with chicken & bok choy in warm broth ([recipe 28](recipes.md#28-rice-noodles-with-chicken--bok-choy-in-warm-broth-鸡菜汤河粉)).
- From **Day 4 (Thu 29 Oct) breakfast** onward, run the table exactly as written, full day.

> **Shopping consequence:** Week 1 home shopping is Days 3–7, not Days 1–7, and **Day 3 lunch items are now must-haves, not optional** — see [`shopping-list.md`](shopping-list.md).

## 🔧 How you'll actually cook it (set-and-forget)

Every dish runs on the **four things actually on the counter** — and the pan work is short and beginner-safe: vegetable sides, plus the sear that starts each braise or soup. Button names here are the ones printed on your machines; [`recipes.md`](recipes.md) has the full method map and the button-by-button table.

- **Joyoung pressure cooker** → rice (**精煮饭**), congee (**浓香粥**), soups (**美味汤**), and the long cook for braises and seasoned chicken (**鸡鸭肉 / 排骨 / 牛羊肉**), finished with **开盖收汁** to reduce the sauce. **开盖收汁 is a reducer, not a sear** — the browning happens in the pan first.
- **Frying pan + the wire rack that lives inside it, lid on** → every steamed dish (fish, egg custard, chicken). The pan is shallow, so anything steaming over 12 min gets a **boiling-water top-up at the 10-minute mark**.
- **AUX air-fryer oven** → fish dinners on the **烤鱼** preset, sweet potato on **红薯**, plus reheating.
- **The same frying pan, rack out (gas hob)** → the quick sear that starts braises, soups and sesame-oil chicken; vegetable sides; noodles; poached eggs; the red-date tea.

> ⏰ **The overnight trick:** the cooker's **预约** button is the least-used, most useful thing in this kitchen with a newborn. Load rice / congee / tea at 10 pm, set it to finish at 6.30 am, and breakfast is cooking while you sleep. **Grains, water and dried herbs only** — never meat, fish or egg in a delayed pot. Details in [`recipes.md`](recipes.md).

> 💡 **Most greens are your choice — stir-fried or blanched, whichever you feel like** (see [`recipes.md`](recipes.md): recipes 53–65 are *originally stir-fried* with an optional blanch; recipe 66 is the deliberately-blanched version). The stir-fry runs on *medium* heat (gentle, no splatter) and takes the same ~2–3 min as blanching, so neither is slower or riskier. The heavy, time-consuming work (rice, soups, braises, fish) stays set-and-forget in the electric appliances.

> 🍶 **Wine dishes are marked 🍶** — the ones with 150–250 ml of 米酒/黄酒 ([recipes 1](recipes.md#1-sesame-oil-chicken-麻油鸡), [4](recipes.md#4-yellow-wine-chicken-黄酒鸡), [69](recipes.md#69-angkak-chicken-红曲米鸡), plus the sesame-oil pork on day 10). Most of them land at **lunch** in this plan, so use the other half of the rule on those days: **have her feed or pump just before the meal, then eat** — that's the practical version of "wait ~2 hours after". Use low-alcohol confinement 米酒, and keep the 20–30 min pressure cook (it drives off roughly 30% of the alcohol, not all of it). Cooking them at dinner, when you can, is still the easiest win.

## 📊 Picking dishes by score

Every recipe in [`recipes.md`](recipes.md) is scored out of 5 for **ease** (how effortless — active minutes and attention, not skill), **taste**, and **confinement fit** (how well it nourishes her recovery and milk supply). The full sorted scoreboard is at the end of that file. If you only remember three things:

- **Tired day, still excellent (ease 4–5, fit 5):** baked salmon (35), steamed fish (8), braised lean pork (44), peanut & pork rib soup (7), breakfast porridges (5, 11, 12), red date tea (6), baked sweet potato (68).
- **Best eating (taste 5):** sesame oil chicken (1), beef brisket (49), pork ribs with lotus root & red dates (47), peanut & pork rib soup (7), yellow wine chicken (4), baked salmon (35).
- **Occasional or supporting (fit 3):** ginger fried rice (14), pork belly braises (43, 46), lettuce (54), zucchini (56) — fine for variety, not the backbone of a day.

> 💡 The scores already include the plan's safety caveats: a wine dish can score 4 on fit because of the feed-first rule, and the angkak chicken (69) sits at 4 because it pauses if her bleeding is still heavy.

## Week 1 — Gentle Recovery 排毒 (Days 1–7)

**Focus:** expel lochia, rest digestion, perineal recovery. Light and easy-to-digest only — no heavy tonics.

> ✅ **Optimised for the confirmed vaginal birth (was: delivery method TBD).** No C-section wound, so: **(1)** no snakehead (生鱼) soup course is needed; **(2)** ginger stays at the **full amount written** in every recipe — the "keep ginger milder in week 1" rule was C-section-only; **(3)** no *extra* tonic delay is needed beyond this plan — sesame oil chicken on Day 8, angkak on Day 12 and dang gui in Week 3 stay exactly as scheduled. Do **not** pull tonics/wine dishes into Week 1 either: the Week 1 lightness is about lochia and digestion, not delivery mode, and it still applies.
>
> 🧻 **Perineal tear (minor, 1 stitch) — Week 1 priority is a soft stool.** Straining is what hurts a stitch, so in practice: **daily warm fruit + leafy/fibrous greens + soups/teas (~2–3 L warm fluids) every day, no skipping** — these are already on the plan, for her they are now load-bearing, not optional extras. Protein (fish, chicken, egg at every meal) and vitamin-C vegetables (broccoli, kai lan, bok choy) support tissue repair.

|---|---|---|---|

| **2** | Fish congee (threadfin) | Rice, steamed egg custard, stir-fried choy sum, tofu-mushroom soup | Rice, braised pork collar slices w/ ginger, blanched broccoli, mild fish soup |
| **3** | Ginger red-date porridge + egg | Rice, baked pomfret (air-fry) w/ ginger, stir-fried lettuce, pork rib-corn soup (light) | Rice noodles w/ chicken & bok choy in warm broth |
| **4** | Oatmeal w/ red dates + egg | Rice, steamed chicken breast w/ goji, blanched kai lan, egg-drop soup | Rice, baked salmon (air-fry), stir-fried sweet potato leaves, white fish soup |

**Snacks / teas (week 1):** warm red date tea, warm red date + longan tea, plain congee, warm soy milk (豆奶) with 2 slices ginger. Keep everything light.

> 🍎 **Daily warm fruit (all weeks):** add 1 portion a day as a snack — stewed apple/pear with ginger + a red date, banana at room temperature, or a little papaya. Fibre + vitamin C help with postpartum constipation and iron absorption; always serve warm.

> 🦴 **Daily calcium (all weeks) — target ~1000 mg.** Breastfeeding needs ~1000–1300 mg/day, and soy milk alone does **not** get you there (unfortified 豆奶 is only ~30–75 mg/100 ml). Stack **two or three of these every day** — one cup of soy milk is only ~300 mg, so a single hit can't cover it: **1 cup fortified/high-calcium milk or soy milk (~300 mg)** · **calcium-set tofu, 150–200 g (~250 mg)** · **20 g ikan bilis 江鱼仔 in a soup (~250–300 mg — go light on the soy sauce that day)** · **100 g kai lan / choy sum / bok choy cooked (~100–150 mg)**. Note that **spinach is oxalate-rich and a poor calcium source** — it's on the plan for iron and folate, not calcium. Short on the tally? **Calcium 500–600 mg + vitamin D3** covers it — ask her doctor.

> 🥚 **Breakfast protein (all weeks):** every breakfast gets a whole egg plus a warm protein drink (soy milk or broth). The paste mornings (days 8, 12, 20, 24) are the easy ones to under-eat — the egg there is part of the meal, not a garnish.

> 🥩 **Week-2 tip:** beef now appears on days 7, **9**, 11 and 25 — day 9's pork lunch has been swapped for **beef w/ ginger & onion**. Beef's iron absorbs better than pork's, so pair any red meat with a vitamin-C vegetable (broccoli, kai lan).

> 🍚 **Mum's angkak (红曲米) — days 12 and 25.** A fermented rice used in small spoonfuls for colour and flavour; 红糟鸡 is a classic confinement chicken and a nice change from the same wine dish. How to use it safely:
> - **Culinary amounts only — 1–2 tsp per pot.** It's a seasoning here, not a supplement (angkak contains monacolin K, the same compound as lovastatin, so it only behaves like medicine at gram-a-day doses).
> - **Buy sealed, properly labelled packs** from a medical hall or supermarket — cheap loose angkak can carry citrinin, a mould toxin. Mum's pack: check it's labelled, then keep it in the fridge.
> - **Pause it if lochia is still heavy or bright red** — 红曲 is 活血 (blood-moving), the same rule as dang gui. That's why it starts in week 2, not week 1.
> - **Cook it with 米酒/黄酒 — never arak.** Arak is a distilled 30–40% spirit and doesn't belong in a breastfeeding kitchen this month ([recipe 69](recipes.md#69-angkak-chicken-红曲米鸡) has a zero-alcohol version).
