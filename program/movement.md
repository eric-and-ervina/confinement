# 🤸 Movement & Pelvic-Floor Recovery

Everything physical in the program, in order, with sets and progressions. The rule that governs all of it:

> ⚕️ **Only what's cleared, only what's comfortable.** Gentle walking and breathing from day 1. Everything else waits for the **6-week check** and the doctor's OK — and core work ideally waits for a women's-health physiotherapist to check the tummy gap and pelvic floor. **Stop immediately** if something causes pain, heavy bleeding, leaking urine/poo, or a bulging/heavy feeling in the vagina.

---

## Why this order (and why the old advice was wrong)

Pregnancy stretches the **pelvic floor** (the sling of muscle holding the bladder, womb and bowel) and the **linea alba** (the seam down the middle of the belly). After birth they're stretched and often weak — but sometimes *too tight and guarding*, not too loose. So the old advice — "do 100 crunches and squeeze your pelvic floor hard all day" — is wrong on both counts:

- **Crunches, sit-ups, planks, and heavy lifting** early on push the belly contents outward against a weak seam and can worsen a **diastasis** (abdominal separation) and pelvic-floor pressure.
- **Maximal, constant pelvic-floor squeezes** can create a tight, painful floor that can't relax — which causes leaking and pain too.

The correct sequence is: **breathe → wake the deep core and pelvic floor gently → rebuild strength → then load**.

---

## Phase 0 — Days 1–7: circulation, breathing, rest

You are preventing clots and letting things heal. That's it.

| Exercise | How | Dose |
|---|---|---|
| **Ankle pumps & circles** | Point and flex the feet, roll the ankles | Every hour you're awake — best clot-prevention move there is (along with early walking) |
| **Diaphragmatic breathing** | One hand on the belly; breathe so the *belly and ribs* widen on the inhale, not the shoulders | 5 slow breaths, 4–6× a day |
| **Pelvic tilt** | Lying down, gently flatten the lower back, then release | 5–8 reps, 2× a day |
| **Stand & walk** | Around the house, or to the corridor and back | As comfortable, several times a day |
| **Roll to get up** | Log-roll to your side, push up with the arms — don't sit straight up | Every time |

**C-section:** also **splint the wound** — hold a small pillow or your hands over the scar when you cough, sneeze, laugh or get up. No lifting heavier than the baby + car seat. Keep the wound clean and dry; massage starts only when the doctor says it's healed (usually ~6 weeks) — see [`appearance.md`](appearance.md).

**Everything warm, nothing straining.** If bleeding gets heavier or redder after activity, you did too much — rest and try less.

## Phase 1 — Weeks 2–6: wake the deep core & pelvic floor

Still no crunches, planks, running, or lifting. This is gentle *re-education*.

### The daily 5-minute pelvic-floor & breath routine

Do this lying down or seated, 1–2× a day.

1. **360° breath (3 breaths).** Inhale: feel the ribs widen sideways and the pelvic floor *soften/descend*. Exhale: feel the ribs knit down and the pelvic floor *gently lift*. **The pelvic floor should move with the breath** — that's the whole trick.
2. **Pelvic-floor "elevator" (5 reps).** On the exhale, gently lift as if stopping wind and holding urine, *at about 30% effort* — a light lift, not a death-grip. Hold 3–5 seconds, then **fully release for 5 seconds.** The release matters as much as the lift.
3. **Heel slides (8 each side).** Lying down, slide one heel out and in, keeping the lower back and pelvis still.
4. **Side-lying clamshells (8 each side)** — only if comfortable: lie on your side, knees bent, open and close the top knee like a clamshell; don't roll the pelvis back.
5. **Posture reset (below).**

### Posture reset (do it after every feed)

| Move | How | Dose |
|---|---|---|
| **Chest opener** | Stand in a doorway, forearms on the frame, step through gently | 20–30 sec |
| **Chin tuck** | Draw the chin straight back (make a "double chin"), hold 5 sec | 10 reps |
| **Wall angel** | Back to a wall, arms slide up and down while touching the wall | 8 reps |
| **Neck release** | Ear to shoulder, gentle hold | 20 sec each side |

*Why:* feeding and carrying a baby curls the shoulders and juts the head forward ("mommy hunch"), which causes the neck/upper-back pain most new mothers get. This 3-minute routine prevents most of it.

### Walking ladder (weeks 2–6)

| Week | Walk |
|---|---|
| 2 | 5–10 min, 1–2× a day |
| 3 | 10–15 min |
| 4 | 15–20 min |
| 5 | 20–25 min |
| 6 | 20–30 min, most days |

Flat ground, supportive shoes, no hills yet.

## The 6-week check — what to ask for

Go in with this list. Ask the doctor (and ask for a referral to a **women's-health physiotherapist** if anything is off):

- [ ] "Can I start core and strength work? Any restrictions?"
- [ ] "Please check my tummy for **diastasis recti**." *(A gap >2 finger-widths, or a doming/bulge when you lift your head, means: rehab first, no crunches/planks yet.)*
- [ ] "Can I have a **pelvic-floor assessment**?" *(Leaking, heaviness, pain, or a bulge = physio referral, not "just keep doing Kegels".)*
- [ ] "Is my wound/tear healed enough for scar massage?"
- [ ] "Are my **iron/ferritin, vitamin D, and thyroid** okay?"
- [ ] "How's my blood pressure / mood?"

> 💡 Many doctors don't automatically check the pelvic floor or the tummy gap. **It is completely normal to ask** — and a physio can give you an internal or real-time ultrasound assessment that a GP can't.

## Phase 2 — Weeks 6–12: rebuild (after clearance)

Order matters: **core first, then strength, then cardio.** Add one thing at a time and give it a week before adding more.

### A. Core rehab (start here) — 3× a week

| Exercise | How | Start with |
|---|---|---|
| **Deep-core breath** | Exhale and gently lift the pelvic floor + draw the lower belly in; keep breathing | 8 breaths |
| **Dead bug** | Lying on your back, arms and legs up; lower one arm/leg away slowly while the belly stays flat and the back stays still | 6 each side |
| **Glute bridge** | Lie on your back, knees bent, lift the hips; squeeze glutes, ribs down | 10–12 reps |
| **Bird dog** | On all fours, extend the opposite arm and leg without letting the pelvis tilt | 6–8 each side |
| **Side plank from knees** | On your side, from the knees, lift the hips; keep the body in a line | 3 × 15–20 sec each side |
| **Farmer carry (light)** | Hold a weight in each hand, walk tall, ribs down | 2 × 20–30 m |

**Avoid until a physio clears you:** crunches, sit-ups, full planks, leg raises, Russian twists, and heavy overhead/loaded lifts. **Doming** (the belly bulging in a ridge down the middle) during any move = stop that move and reduce the difficulty.

### B. Strength — from week 9, 2–3× a week

Rebuilds muscle, metabolism, bone density, and posture. Use bodyweight first, then light dumbbells (start 2–4 kg).

| Pattern | Example | Sets |
|---|---|---|
| Squat | Bodyweight squat → goblet squat | 2–3 × 10 |
| Hip hinge | Glute bridge → Romanian deadlift (light) | 2–3 × 10 |
| Push | Wall push-up → incline push-up | 2–3 × 8–10 |
| Pull | Resistance-band row → dumbbell row | 2–3 × 10 |
| Carry / core | Farmer carry, side plank, dead bug | 2–3 sets |
| Posture | Wall angel, band pull-apart | 2 × 12 |

Two full-body sessions a week is plenty in Phase 2. Consistency beats intensity.

### C. Cardio — build to 150 min/week

Brisk walking, stationary bike, or swimming (once the doctor clears it and bleeding has stopped). Start with what Phase 1 built and add ~10% a week. **Talk-test pace:** you can speak in short sentences.

### D. Return to running / jumping

**Only after a women's-health physio screen says yes**, usually no earlier than ~12 weeks and often longer. The test: **no leaking, no heaviness/bulge, no pelvic pain, and good single-leg strength** (a 30-second single-leg balance and a set of single-leg bridges without wobbling). Running too early is one of the most common causes of long-term leaking. If in doubt, wait.

## Phase 3 — Months 3–12: return to full

| Goal | Target |
|---|---|
| Strength | 3×/week progressive full-body: squat, hinge, push, pull, carry |
| Cardio | 150 min/week moderate, or 75 min vigorous (once cleared) |
| Pelvic floor | Keep the daily breath + elevator routine **forever** — it protects against leaking at every stage of life |
| Impact | Run, jump, play, lift — as cleared and as comfortable |
| Posture | Keep the post-feed reset; raise the baby-carrying ergonomics (see [`appearance.md`](appearance.md)) |

## Exercise safety rules (print these to the fridge)

1. **Bleeding gets heavier/redder after activity → you did too much.** Rest.
2. **Pain, pressure, heaviness, or bulging in the vagina → stop and see a women's-health physio.**
3. **Leaking urine on a cough/sneeze/lift → stop the exercise; it needs assessing, not pushing through.**
4. **No crunches, sit-ups, full planks, or heavy lifts until the tummy gap and pelvic floor are cleared.**
5. **Never exercise to exhaustion.** With a newborn, "tired" is the baseline; training should leave you more capable, not wiped out.
6. **Hydrate and eat.** Exercise while breastfeeding needs water and enough calories (see the [meal plan](../meal/meal-plan.md)).
7. **C-section:** no lifting beyond baby + car seat for the first ~6 weeks, no abdominal work until cleared, and stop if the wound stings or pulls.
