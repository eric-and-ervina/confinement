# ✨ Appearance Care — Skin, Hair, Marks & Body

The appearance side of recovery. The reassuring truth first:

> 🌿 **Most postpartum appearance changes are caused by hormones and time — which means they mostly improve on their own, over months.** Sleep, food, sun protection and consistency do more than any expensive product. The program's job is to **support** that recovery and avoid the things that interfere with it (crash dieting, harsh products, sun, unregulated "slimming" or whitening products).

> ⚕️ **Breastfeeding-safe?** Ask her doctor or a dermatologist about any active ingredient if she is breastfeeding or pregnant again. The general safe list below is a starting point, not a prescription.

## The daily glow routine (5 minutes, morning + night)

| | Morning | Night |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Gentle cleanser** (or just warm water if skin is dry) | **Gentle cleanser** |
| 2 | **Vitamin C** or **niacinamide** serum (optional, if oily/pigmenting) | **Azelaic acid** (pigment + acne) or **hyaluronic acid** (if dry) |
| 3 | **Moisturiser** | **Moisturiser** (slightly richer) |
| 4 | **Broad-spectrum SPF 30–50** — the single most important step | — |

That's it. Consistency beats a 10-step routine, and with a newborn, 5 minutes is the realistic unit.

**Breastfeeding-safe actives:** azelaic acid, niacinamide, vitamin C, hyaluronic acid, glycerin, ceramides, glycolic/lactic acid (mild), zinc oxide sunscreen.
**Ask the doctor first / usually avoid while breastfeeding:** tretinoin and other retinoids, high-dose salicylic peels, hydroquinone. (These are fine to add once she's stopped breastfeeding.)

## Melasma — the "mask of pregnancy"

Dark, blotchy patches on the cheeks, forehead, upper lip or jaw. Very common in Singapore's sun and more common in deeper skin tones.

- **Cause:** pregnancy hormones + **UV light**. UV is the trigger that keeps it going.
- **What actually works:**
  1. **Sun protection, seriously.** Broad-spectrum SPF 30–50, reapplied every 2–3 hours outdoors, plus a **wide-brim hat and shade**. Sunscreen alone can fade melasma over months.
  2. **Safe brighteners:** azelaic acid, niacinamide, vitamin C, kojic acid — mild but they help.
  3. **Pigment-safe:** avoid waxing/heat on the face if it triggers it, and never pick at the skin.
  4. **Time.** It often improves significantly over months as hormones settle, especially once breastfeeding ends.
- **For stubborn cases:** a dermatologist can prescribe stronger treatment (including hydroquinone) **after** breastfeeding, or do gentle lasers. Don't buy over-the-counter "whitening" products — see the warning below.

---

## 🚫 The one big warning — "whitening" and "slimming" products

Southeast Asia's markets are full of **unregulated skin-lightening creams and "slimming" supplements**. Some creams contain **mercury**, **strong steroids**, or banned hydroquinone — they can cause permanent skin damage, stretch marks, and serious health harm; a few "slimming" products have contained banned drugs.

**Rules:**
- Buy skincare from a **pharmacy or reputable brand**, not a mystery tub from a chat group.
- If a cream whitens dramatically within days, be suspicious.
- **No weight-loss pills, teas, or "detox" while breastfeeding** — nothing beats food and movement here.
- Botox, fillers and most injectables: **ask the doctor**; generally wait until after breastfeeding.

## Acne & oiliness

Pregnancy acne usually settles after delivery, as hormones fall. If it lingers:
- Gentle cleanser twice a day, don't scrub.
- **Azelaic acid** (safe, treats both acne and pigment) and **niacinamide**.
- Small amounts of **benzoyl peroxide** on spots are generally considered okay in limited areas while breastfeeding — ask the doctor.
- Change pillowcases often; keep hair off the face.
- **Don't pick** — it causes the very marks you're trying to avoid.

## Dryness, sensitivity & "postpartum skin"

Skin can be dry, reactive, and dull from hormones, dehydration, and no sleep.
- Barrier-repair moisturiser with **ceramides**; a **humidifier** in the room.
- Lukewarm showers, not hot; gentle, fragrance-free cleanser.
- **Hydrate** — it helps skin, energy and milk. Aim for ~2–3 L of fluids/day, warm (see the [meal plan](../meal/meal-plan.md)).

## Linea nigra, darkened areolae, underarms & neck

The dark line down the belly, darker nipples, and darker creases are **normal hormone pigmentation**. They **fade slowly over 6–12 months** and need no treatment. Keep them out of the sun (SPF), moisturise, and be patient.

## Puffiness, dark circles & the "tired face"

Usually sleep, salt, and fluid retention — plus, sometimes, **iron deficiency or thyroid changes**, which the 6-week bloods check.
- Prioritise sleep (see [`mind-body.md`](mind-body.md)); elevate the head slightly.
- Reduce salty processed food; keep the fluid intake up.
- **Dark circles are mostly genetics + tiredness** — no cream erases them. Concealer, water, sleep.

## Hair — postpartum shedding (the scariest-looking, most temporary one)

**What happens:** during pregnancy, high hormones keep hairs in the growing phase, so hair looks thick and lush. After birth, hormones drop, and all that extra hair **sheds at once — usually starting around month 3 and peaking around months 4–5, then slowing by months 6–12.** It comes out in handfuls in the shower and on the pillow. **It is normal and it regrows.** New short "baby hairs" at the hairline are the regrowth.

**What actually helps:**
- **Do nothing drastic.** No harsh treatments, no panic.
- **Check iron/ferritin, thyroid, and vitamin D** (6-week bloods) — treat any deficiency. Iron deficiency makes shedding worse.
- **Eat enough protein** — hair is protein (the meal plan's eggs, fish, chicken and soups cover this).
- **Gentle care:** wide-tooth comb, avoid tight buns/braids and harsh bleaching, limit heat styling, wash when needed.
- **Biotin** only helps if she's genuinely deficient; it's not a cure and can distort thyroid blood tests.

**What doesn't help:** expensive "hair growth" shampoos, mass-market supplements, and stressing about it. It's a hormone crash, and it reverses itself.

## Stretch marks (striae gravidarum)

Affect more than half of pregnancies. Fresh ones are **red/purple** (that's the treatable stage); over time they become **silver and flatter** but may never vanish completely.

- **Moisturise + massage** daily (centella, hyaluronic acid or a simple rich cream) — helps the skin feel better and may marginally help.
- **Keep them out of the sun** — sun darkens them and makes them more visible.
- **Retinoids** (only after breastfeeding) modestly improve *fresh, red* marks.
- **Dermatologist options:** microneedling, fractional laser, and other treatments for stubborn marks — discuss cost and expectations honestly.
- **Acceptance is part of the medicine.** These marks fade into something you barely notice with time; the goal is healthy skin, not airbrushed skin.

## C-section scar

- **Weeks 0–6:** keep clean and dry, no massage yet; let it heal. Watch for redness/hot/oozing (see red flags).
- **Once the doctor says healed (~6 weeks):** gentle **scar massage** (small circles, 5 min, once a day) to stop it sticking to the deeper tissue, and **silicone sheets/gel** to help the scar flatten and fade.
- **Keloid (raised scar), more common in deeper skin tones:** silicone + massage help; ask a dermatologist if it's raised and itchy.
- Realistic timeline: the scar fades from red/pink to pale over **1–2 years**.

## Teeth & gums

Pregnancy raises the risk of **gum disease** (pregnancy gingivitis) and, with disrupted brushing, cavities.
- **See the dentist** in the first few months — it's safe while breastfeeding.
- **Brush twice a day, floss once**, and use fluoride toothpaste. If gums bleed, that's inflammation, not "normal" — get it checked.
- Keep **calcium and vitamin D** up (the [meal plan](../meal/meal-plan.md) has calcium boosters).

## Feet

The hormone **relaxin** loosens ligaments, so arches can flatten and the foot can grow up to **half a size or more** — often permanently.
- Wear **properly fitting, supportive shoes** — not thin flip-flops all day.
- Arch support insoles help arch/heel pain.
- Don't squeeze into old shoes; that causes heel and toe pain.

## Varicose & spider veins, and haemorrhoids

Caused by pregnancy's extra blood volume and pressure, and they improve but may not disappear.
- **Compression stockings** (ask the doctor for the right class), **elevate the legs**, keep moving, and avoid standing still for long.
- Haemorrhoids: **high-fibre food + fluids** (the meal plan's vegetables, fruit and oats), don't strain, and use a footstool so knees sit above hips. Persistent ones → see a doctor.
- Spider veins are cosmetic; treat later with a dermatologist if desired.

## Posture & "mommy thumb"

- Feeding and carrying cause neck, upper-back and shoulder pain — do the **post-feed posture reset** in [`movement.md`](movement.md) after every feed.
- **De Quervain's ("mommy thumb"):** wrist/thumb pain from lifting the baby. Use a **thumb-spica brace**, lift with both hands and the whole forearm, keep the wrist neutral, and see a doctor/physio if it persists. It's common and treatable.
- Raise the baby, don't lower yourself: feed with **pillows/support** so she isn't hunched, and change nappies at a comfortable height.

## Weight & shape — the honest section

- **Loss of ~5–6 kg** at delivery is normal, then a gradual, slow decline with good food and movement.
- **Never below ~1,800 kcal/day while breastfeeding** (milk needs ~330–400 extra kcal/day). Crash diets cut milk, hair, mood and energy — and backfire.
- **Aim for ≤0.5 kg/week** once past week 6 and only if she wants to; the first 3 months are for healing, not shrinking.
- **Her shape may change permanently** — ribs, hips and the waist shift. That's bone and structure, not laziness. The goal is a strong, well body, not the teenage one.
- **Don't compare** to celebrity "postpartum bodies" — those are staged, airbrushed, paid-for, and often involve money and teams nobody has.
