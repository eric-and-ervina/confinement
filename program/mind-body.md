# 🧠 Mind, Sleep & Pelvic Health

The pillar most often ignored — and the one that quietly drives everything else: mood, energy, milk, skin, and how she feels in her own body. This document covers sleep, mood (including postpartum depression and anxiety), intimacy and pelvic health, breastfeeding body care, and how to build the support net around her.

> ⚕️ **Mental health is health.** If either of you is worried, talk to the doctor. Asking for help early is the fastest path back, not a failure.

---

## 😴 Sleep — the highest-leverage medicine

Sleep deprivation worsens mood, skin, healing, milk supply, and the risk of postpartum depression. It is not a "nice to have" — it's the treatment.

**Build sleep on purpose:**

1. **"Sleep when the baby sleeps"** — meaning it. Even a 20-minute lie-down counts. Resist the urge to clean instead.
2. **Shift the night.** If she's breastfeeding, you (the partner) take one wake-up — bring the baby to her for a feed, then **take the baby away to burp, change and settle** while she goes straight back to sleep. If she pumps, you can take a whole feed with expressed milk.
3. **Protect one 4-hour block.** A single uninterrupted 4-hour stretch of sleep does more for mood than eight broken hours. Swap nights or swap shifts to make it happen.
4. **Keep the room dark, cool and quiet**; phone out of the bed.
5. **Naps beat caffeine.** A 20-minute afternoon nap helps more than a third coffee — and coffee passes into milk.
6. **Tag-team with a family member or a night nanny** for a night or two a week in the hardest early weeks, if at all possible.

> 💡 **Partner's job description, in one line:** guard her sleep, feed her well, and take the baby so she can shower, eat, and rest. That is worth more than any product on the market.

## 💙 Mood — baby blues vs postpartum depression/anxiety

| | **Baby blues** | **Postpartum depression (PPD) / anxiety** |
|---|---|---|
| When | Days 3–10 | Any time in the first year, often weeks 4–12 |
| How long | Days, up to ~2 weeks, then lifts | **More than 2 weeks**, and/or worsening |
| Feelings | Tearful, overwhelmed, moody, then okay | Persistent sadness, numbness, guilt, hopelessness, rage, can't bond, no interest in things she loved |
| Anxiety | Some worry | Racing thoughts, dread, intrusive scary thoughts, can't sleep even when the baby sleeps, checking the baby constantly |
| What to do | Rest, support, reassurance | **See the doctor.** Talk therapy and medication (many are breastfeeding-safe) work well |

**Warning signs that mean "call the doctor today":**
- Low mood **more days than not for over 2 weeks**.
- Can't enjoy anything, can't bond, or feels like her family would be better off without her.
- Not eating, not sleeping even when the baby sleeps, or unable to function.
- Panic attacks, or intrusive frightening thoughts (these are common and **treatable** — not a sign she's dangerous).

### 🚨 Postpartum psychosis — a medical emergency
Confusion, paranoia, hearing/seeing things, bizarre beliefs, or **any thought of harming herself or the baby**. **Call 995 or go to A&E immediately.** This is rare, serious, and treatable — never something to "wait out."

**The EPDS:** the Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale is a 10-question screening tool most clinics use. Ask her doctor or midwife to do one at the 6-week check and again at 3 months (and any time she's struggling). Score ≥10–13 usually means "let's look closer." **Question 10 is about self-harm — any score above zero there needs immediate help.**

**Small daily mood medicine:** daylight and a short walk, one honest conversation, a few minutes that are just hers, and enough sleep. Track mood weekly in [`tracker.md`](tracker.md) so patterns are visible instead of overwhelming.

## 🌸 Pelvic health & intimacy

- **Dryness and low libido are normal** — prolactin (the milk hormone) and low oestrogen cause both, and this is temporary.
- **Wait until cleared** (usually ~6 weeks) **and until she actually wants to.** "Cleared" means safe, not ready. If a tear or C-section is still sore, wait longer.
- **Take it slowly.** Use **lubricant** (water- or silicone-based); moisturisers and longer foreplay help. Pain is not something to push through — it means slow down or stop.
- **If sex hurts, or if she feels heaviness/bulging/pain in the vagina**, see a **women's-health physiotherapist** and/or doctor. Pelvic-floor tension, scarring and prolapse are all treatable — and pelvic-floor physio is highly effective.
- **Leaking urine** (on a cough, sneeze, run) is **common but not something to live with** — see a physio. It's fixable.
- **Contraception:** fertility can return **as early as ~6 weeks**, even while breastfeeding, and **breastfeeding is not reliable contraception**. Plan this at the 6-week check.

## 🤱 Breastfeeding body care

- **Engorgement:** feed often, cold packs between feeds, gentle hand-expression for comfort.
- **Blocked duct:** massage toward the nipple, feed on that side, warm compress before feeds.
- **Mastitis** — a hot, red, wedge-shaped, painful breast **with fever or flu-like aches** — needs **prompt medical care**; keep feeding/pumping on that side and see a doctor quickly.
- **Nipple care:** correct latch first, then **lanolin** for broken skin; let the breasts air-dry and change pads often.
- **Nursing can be hard**; if it hurts, a **lactation consultant** (IBCLC) is worth their fee many times over. Singapore: hospitals, polyclinics, and the breastfeeding support groups can refer.
- **If she chooses to stop or can't breastfeed** — formula is a perfectly good answer. Fed is fed. Guilt has no place here.

## 🫂 The support network she actually needs

A new mother should not be doing this alone. Build the net before it's needed:

| Need | Who can do it |
|---|---|
| **Sleep** | Partner on a night shift; a family member one night a week |
| **Food** | Partner cooking the [meal plan](../meal/meal-plan.md); friends on a meal train |
| **Baby care** | Anyone she trusts — so she can shower, eat, rest |
| **Housework** | Paid cleaner, family, or partner. Outsource what you can afford |
| **Emotional support** | Partner first; then a friend, mother, sibling, or therapist |
| **Professional support** | OB/GP, women's-health physio, lactation consultant, and a counsellor/therapist |
| **Company** | Other new mothers (hospital groups, local mama meet-ups, online communities) |

**What helps her most, in her own words, is usually simple:** "Can I take the baby for an hour so you can rest?" — asked often, and meant.

## Relationship, identity & the long game

- The early months are survival. **Lower the bar on everything but each other and the baby.**
- A daily **5-minute check-in** with each other (how are you really?) prevents most resentment.
- She is not only a mother. Protect **one thing that is hers** — a shower, a walk, a hobby, a friend, a book.
- It's normal to grieve the old body, the old life, the couple you were. That grief doesn't mean anything is wrong — it means something real changed. Health and joy come back, in a new shape.
