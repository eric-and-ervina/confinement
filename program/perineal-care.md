# 🧻 Perineal Tear & Stitch Care

**Written for her actual birth: unmedicated vaginal delivery, one stitch (a minor tear).** This is the wound the rest of the program sometimes forgets — there is no C-section scar to look at, so the tear is easy to under-care for. It is real tissue, it is healing, and for the first two weeks it is the thing most likely to make her uncomfortable.

The meal plan already covers the *inside* half of this: **a soft stool**. Warm fruit, fibre greens and ~2–3 L of warm fluids every day are in the day tables for exactly this reason. This document covers the *outside* half — hygiene, pressure, pain and the "is this normal?" questions.

> ⚕️ **Not medical advice.** Tear repair, pain relief and stool softeners are the doctor's or midwife's call — ask at discharge, or at the 6-week check. **Get help now** if pain is getting worse instead of better, if she has a fever, if the wound looks open or smells wrong, or if she can't pass urine (see the red flags below).

---

## What one stitch actually means

- A **single stitch** usually means a **first-degree tear** (skin only), or a small **second-degree** one where the muscle was stitched with one or two sutures. It is the mildest end of the range — a good thing.
- **The stitches are dissolvable.** They do not need removing; they dissolve on their own, typically within **1–2 weeks** (some types take up to ~4). A loose stitch thread appearing on the pad around day 5–10 is normal — leave it alone.
- **Skin heals in ~1–2 weeks; the deeper tissue takes ~4–6 weeks.** So she may feel "fine" by day 10 and still get a twinge at week 4. That is expected, not a setback.
- **It often hurts *more* on days 3–5 than on day 1** — swelling, bruising and the tissues waking up. Day 1–2 is frequently the calmest. Warn her ahead of time so it doesn't frighten her.

---

## First 48 hours

1. **Ice, on-and-off.** A wrapped cold pack or a chilled gel pad **over** the pad (never directly on skin) for **10–20 minutes every 2–3 hours** on day 1–2 keeps swelling and the throbbing down. It also numbs the area.
2. **Pain relief, kept ahead of the pain.** Paracetamol and (if cleared) ibuprofen are generally breastfeeding-safe — **confirm the exact drugs and doses with her doctor**, and take them *on schedule* for the first few days rather than waiting for it to hurt.
3. **Warm water after every toilet trip.** See the hygiene loop below.
4. **Let the area breathe** when she can lie down — pads off, a towel or maternity pad under her, fresh air for 10–20 minutes, a few times a day.

---

## The hygiene loop (every toilet trip, first 2 weeks)

1. **Peri bottle, warm water, front to back.** Fill a peri (squeeze) bottle with **warm** water and rinse after every pee and poo. Warm water cleans, soothes and reduces stinging. A spare bottle in each bathroom helps.
2. **Pat, never wipe.** Blot dry with tissue front-to-back (or air-dry), then change to a **fresh pad**.
3. **Change the pad every 2–4 hours** — and always after a poo — even if it doesn't look full. Damp, warm, blood-soaked pads are how infection starts.
4. **Wash her hands before and after.** Front-to-back, always — the same rule as preventing a UTI.

> 🧴 **Do not** use scented soaps, wipes, sprays, douches or "feminine hygiene" products on the tear. Plain warm water, gentle and unscented cleanser on the rest of the body. Nothing goes inside the vagina.

---

## Sitz baths — the single best comfort habit

A **sitz bath** is a shallow warm soak of the perineum. It eases pain, keeps the area clean, and helps the swelling settle.

- **How:** a clean tub or a **sitz bath basin** that sits over the toilet, with **warm (not hot) water** deep enough to cover the perineum. **10–15 minutes, 2–3× a day**, from ~day 2 on.
- **Plain warm water is enough.** A handful of **salt** in the water (or a sitz-bath salt) is a common, harmless addition — **no soap, no antiseptics, no herbs inside it** unless the doctor says so.
- **After:** pat dry gently, fresh pad.
- **Also works:** a warm shower, letting the water run over the area, once the first 24 hours are past.

> 💡 **Ask the midwife before the first one** if the tear was stitched deeply or the doctor gave specific instructions — for a single stitch, warm sitz baths from day 2 are routine and helpful.

## Sitting, moving and pressure

- **Don't sit upright for long.** Long spells on a hard chair put the whole body's weight on the stitch. Lean to one side, sit on a **soft cushion or a donut/pregnancy pillow**, keep the chair time short, and lie down instead when she can.
- **Lie down to feed** where possible in week 1 — side-lying or reclined takes pressure right off the perineum.
- **Roll out of bed** (log-roll to the side, push up with the arms) — no sitting straight up.
- **No heavy lifting** — baby, nappy bag, everything else, pass it over. She should not be lifting the car seat or a toddler for the first ~2 weeks.
- **No squatting, no straining, no bearing down.** If she has to push to poo, stop and fix the stool (below) rather than push through it.

---

## Bowels & bladder — the practical part

**The stool is the thing that hurts the stitch, so this is not a side issue.**

- **Keep it soft:** daily warm fruit, leafy/fibrous greens, oats, and ~2–3 L warm fluids — all already in the [meal plan](../meal/meal-plan.md). Do not skip them.
- **Footstool + knees above hips** on the toilet, and **don't hold your breath or strain**. Relax, let it happen, don't rush.
- **If it's still hard:** a **stool softener** (e.g. docusate) or fibre supplement — **ask the doctor/pharmacist which is safe while breastfeeding**. Do not use a stimulant laxative without asking, and never an enema.
- **Don't hold it in.** Going promptly, gently and often beats waiting until it's worse.
- **Pee freely.** Stinging on urine is normal early; it improves. **If she can't pass urine at all, or it dribbles constantly, or she can't tell when her bladder is full — tell the doctor the same day** (urinary retention is a real postpartum problem, not something to wait out).

## What to expect, week by week

| When | What's normal |
|---|---|
| **Days 1–2** | Sore, bruised, swollen. Ice, pain relief, rest. Often less painful than expected |
| **Days 3–5** | **Peak soreness and swelling.** Warm sitz baths, ice, keep ahead of the pain. This is the hardest stretch |
| **Days 5–10** | Steadily better. Stitch threads may appear on the pad — normal. Bleeding (lochia) still red/pink |
| **~Day 10–14** | Skin healed, stitches dissolved. Discomfort mostly gone; a twinge when sitting long or walking far |
| **Weeks 2–6** | Deeper tissue continues to heal. **Occasional ache is normal**; increasing pain is not. Lochia fades to brown then yellow-white |
| **~6 weeks** | Reviewed at the check. Should be comfortable by now. Any pain with sitting, sex or exercise → ask about a women's-health physio |

> 🩸 **Lochia (postpartum bleeding) reminder:** it should **fade and slow** over weeks, not get heavier or brighter over time. **Activity that makes bleeding heavier or redder = she did too much** — rest, and do less. Full detail and the difference between normal lochia and a problem is in [`README.md`](README.md#-emergency-red-flags--get-medical-help-now).

## Pelvic floor — gentle, and *not* the same as "do your Kegels hard"

The tear and the pelvic floor are neighbours, and the same rule applies to both: **early on, the goal is comfort and easy circulation, not training.**

- **Ankle pumps, breathing, short walks** — from day 1, as comfortable (see [`movement.md`](movement.md)).
- **No hard, repeated squeezing, no "100 Kegels a day", no bearing down.** Over-working a sore floor makes it tight and painful.
- **Wait for the 6-week assessment** before any pelvic-floor or core *training*, and get a **women's-health physiotherapist** review if there's leaking, heaviness, a bulge, or pain — see [`movement.md`](movement.md).

## 🚨 Perineal red flags — get seen, don't wait

| Symptom | Why |
|---|---|
| **Pain getting worse after day 5**, or sharp pain that was improving | Infection, a haematoma, or a stitch problem |
| **Fever ≥ 38 °C, chills, or feeling unwell** | Infection — needs treatment now |
| **Foul-smelling discharge** (beyond normal lochia), or yellow/green pus | Wound infection |
| **The wound looks open, gaping, or the stitch has pulled out** | Dehiscence — needs review |
| **Increasing swelling, a hard lump, or severe throbbing pain** | Possible haematoma (a collection of blood) |
| **Can't pass urine, or can't tell when the bladder is full** | Urinary retention — same-day problem |
| **A heavy, dragging, or "something coming down" feeling in the vagina** | Pelvic-floor/prolapse concern — physio referral, not "keep going" |
| **Soaking a pad in an hour, or passing large clots** | Postpartum haemorrhage — A&E (see [`README.md`](README.md)) |

**Singapore:** your hospital's maternity ward, your OB's clinic or a polyclinic can review the wound; **995** for anything urgent.

## What to have at home before day 3

| Item | Where | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Peri bottle (squeeze bottle)** | Pharmacy/baby store (often given by the hospital) | One per bathroom if you can |
| **Maternity pads** (unscented, thick) | Pharmacy/supermarket | Change every 2–4 hours — buy plenty |
| **Sitz bath basin** (over-toilet) or a clean shallow tub | Pharmacy | Warm water, 2–3× a day |
| **Reusable gel cold packs** | Pharmacy | Wrapped, over the pad, first 48 h |
| **Donut / ring cushion** | Pharmacy | For unavoidable sitting |
| **Plain paracetamol (+ ibuprofen if cleared)** | Pharmacy | Keep ahead of the pain early on |
| **Stool softener** (if the doctor agrees) | Pharmacy | For the soft-stool rule on the hard days |
| **Unscented gentle wash** | Pharmacy | For everywhere *except* the tear |
